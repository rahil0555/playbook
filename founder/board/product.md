# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF
## What is strong
- It exists and runs. Diagnostics with persistence, lesson resumption, adaptive flows, AI explanations, TTS narration with replay and caching, and a parent view are built. The unglamorous parts (resume where you left off, audio caching) are signs of craft where nobody looks.
- The diagnostic is a natural "first five minutes": a student finishes a short test and sees exactly what they do not know yet. That is a moment worth designing everything back from.
- School-led rollout puts a whole class in front of the product at once, with a teacher present, which is the best setting to watch the experience and fix it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No one thing.** Diagnostics, adaptive paths, AI explanations, voice, mastery tracking, parent visibility, school visibility, plus PlayLab for early years. The pitch needs a paragraph. With four founders and no stated budget, every feature is shallow and none is brilliant. PlayLab is a different user (pre-readers) and a different product; it should not ship now.
2. **Seams between school, teacher, student and parent.** The school signs, the teacher orients, the student uses it, the parent may pay ₹299. Each handover is a place it breaks: a teacher who never opens the school view, a student who stops after onboarding, a parent asked to pay for something the school chose. Who pays is UNKNOWN, so the payment touchpoint itself is undesigned.
3. **AI explanation quality the founders cannot see.** An OpenAI-generated explanation that is wrong, off-syllabus for the board the school follows, or pitched at the wrong level, read aloud by TTS to a child, damages trust with the teacher and parent at once. There is no stated review or quality check. Learning-outcome evidence is UNKNOWN.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Say Edurance in one sentence. Which single feature would you keep if you had to cut the rest?
- Walk me through a Class 7 student's first five minutes after the teacher says "log in". What do they see, and when do they hear the voice?
- What does the teacher do with it on Monday morning, and how long does that take?
- Who pays ₹299, for what period, and what does the parent see before being asked?
- How do you check that AI explanations are correct and match the school's syllabus and language of instruction?
- What is AI API cost per active student per month? (UNKNOWN today.)
- Why is PlayLab being built before one school has shown sustained use?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **One sentence, one core loop.** Founders commit in writing to one grade band, one subject and one loop (diagnose, explain, practise, re-test); PlayLab and non-core features frozen until condition 3 is met.
2. **Observed first session.** In at least 2 pilot schools, a full class completes onboarding and the diagnostic in one period without founder help, with at least 80% of students finishing.
3. **Sustained use.** At least 40% of onboarded students active in each of weeks 4 to 6 of the pilot (threshold to be confirmed by the board, since no engagement baseline exists).
4. **Explanation quality.** A sample of at least 100 AI explanations reviewed by teachers, with an error rate below 2% and a defined correction process.
5. **Payer defined.** Who pays, billing period and school share stated, and the payment step tested with real parents or schools before any scale-up.
