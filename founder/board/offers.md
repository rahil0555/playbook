# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The dream outcome is the right one.** "Close your child's learning gaps" is a large, emotional outcome that parents and schools already care about. The diagnostic gives a natural before-and-after to prove it.
- **The buyers are easy to find.** 150 named schools across four zones, each with a founder who owns it, is a reachable, bounded market. Selling to schools reaches groups of students at once, which beats selling one student at a time.
- **A working product exists.** Diagnostics, adaptive flows, TTS and parent views are built, so the cost of testing an offer is low.
- **The diagnostic is a ready-made entry offer.** A free or cheap baseline report showing each student's gaps is cheap to deliver and valuable to both schools and parents.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof, so low perceived likelihood.** Engagement, retention and learning-outcome evidence are all UNKNOWN. Without results, a school has no reason to believe the outcome will happen, and price becomes the only thing anyone talks about.
2. **Unclear buyer, and a lot of effort asked.** Nobody has said who pays the ₹299, or for what period. The school is asked to give meetings, sign proposals, run educator orientation and onboard students. If the school does all that work and the parent pays, the offer asks a lot of one party and gives the money to another.
3. **A commodity offer at a low price.** "AI explanations plus narration" is a list of features, not a named result. At ₹299, with the revenue share and AI API cost per student UNKNOWN, there may be no margin left to fund the onboarding and support that schools will expect.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who pays the ₹299, for what period, and what does the school receive in return?
- What specific result can you promise in how many weeks, for example "measurable gain on the follow-up diagnostic within one term"?
- Would you offer a guarantee, such as a refund or a free extra term if the follow-up diagnostic shows no gain? Can you afford it?
- What is the AI API cost per active student per month?
- What does a school have to do, in hours of staff time, to get students active?
- What would make a principal feel stupid saying no: a free baseline gap report for every class? A teacher dashboard? Parent reports?
- Could you charge double and still be the obvious choice? If not, why not?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Payer and terms defined in writing.** The payer (school or parent), billing period and school revenue share are fixed before the next pilot is signed.
2. **Pilots signed.** At least 3 of the 150 schools sign structured pilots within 90 days, each with a named decision-maker and an agreed success metric.
3. **Usage holds.** At least 50% of onboarded pilot students are active weekly in week 6.
4. **Outcome proof.** Pilot students show a measurable gain from baseline to follow-up diagnostic over one term, reported per school.
5. **Someone pays.** At least one pilot converts to paid at ₹299, with the actual conversion rate recorded.
6. **Unit economics known.** AI API cost plus support cost per paid user is measured and below net revenue per user after the revenue share.
7. **The offer has a name and a guarantee.** A named, outcome-based offer (diagnostic, plan, progress reports, guarantee) is tested against the plain feature pitch in at least 10 school meetings.
